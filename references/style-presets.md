# Style Presets — Register Targets for FULL Mode

Nine presets: **Formal, Akademik, Profesional, Kasual, Diplomatis, Sekolah, Simple, Percaya Diri, Marketing**.

**Rules**
- In FULL mode, lock ONE preset **before** Stage 3 (style pass). User names it → use it. Context unambiguous (thesis → Akademik; office email → Profesional; landing page → Marketing) → infer and state it in the change log. Ambiguous + high stakes → ask ONE short question with 2–3 best-fit options.
- A preset shapes *how* things are said (tone, rhythm, voice, formatting) — never *what* is true. Meaning lock, anti-hallucination, and the grammar gates always override the preset: **Percaya Diri and Marketing never license invented claims, fake proof, or overclaiming.**
- Mechanics follow the language's grammar protocol (`protocols/grammar-<lang>.md`); register follows the preset.
- Never apply two presets at once. "Formal tapi santai" / "professional but friendly" → nearest preset (Profesional), noted as a blend.
- Foreign/technical terms stay as they are per `protocols/grammar-id.md` §3.1 — no preset licenses translating established terms into descriptive phrases (except Simple/Sekolah, and only with a precise one-time gloss).

## Overview

| Preset | Stance | Sentence rhythm | Voice | AI-tell watchlist |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|-------------------|
| Formal | official, courteous | complete, balanced | impersonal, passive OK | bureaucratic scaffolding |
| Akademik | evidence-led, cautious | varied, precise | hedged, attributed | significance inflation, template openers |
| Profesional | direct, action-first | short, subject-first | "saya/we" sparing | padding, corporate buzzwords |
| Kasual | conversational, warm | loose, contractions | 1st/2nd person | forced slang, emoji spam |
| Diplomatis | face-saving, soft | measured, indirect | suggestions over orders | vagueness drift, over-hedging |
| Sekolah | clear, explanatory | simple, structured | student-authentic | fake sophistication, invented "facts" |
| Simple | plain, accessible | short (≤15 words) | active, concrete | jargon, nominalization, precision loss |
| Percaya Diri | decisive, assertive | punchy, active | strong 1st person | fake certainty, resume hallucination |
| Marketing | persuasive, benefit-led | hook-first, scannable | "kamu/Anda/you"-focused | empty hype, fabricated proof |

## 1. Formal
**Use for**: official letters, announcements, institutional documents, formal complaints.
- Vocabulary: baku ID / elevated EN; no contractions (EN), no colloquialisms (ID).
- Full sentences; politeness formulas where the language requires them; actor omitted only when genuinely irrelevant.
- ID: "Bersama surat ini kami sampaikan bahwa rapat ditunda hingga pemberitahuan lebih lanjut."
- EN: "We are writing to inform you that the meeting has been postponed until further notice."
- Watch: "Berdasarkan hal tersebut di atas..." scaffolding — formality is not padding.
- Banned: emoji, rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, sales language.

## 2. Akademik
**Use for**: theses, journal articles, literature reviews, academic assignments.
- Claims matched to evidence; attribution for non-obvious claims; defined terminology; explicit limitation language.
- Hedging where warranted ("may", "menunjukkan", "suggests"); absolutes only with proof.
- ID: "Temuan ini sejalan dengan hasil studi X (2019), meskipun besaran efeknya berbeda."
- EN: "These results are consistent with X (2019), although the effect size differs."
- Watch: inflated significance ("sangat fundamental"), bold decoration, template openers.
- Academic ≠ stiff: directness and clarity outrank formality theater.

## 3. Profesional
**Use for**: workplace email, reports, proposals, chat messages to colleagues/clients.
- Subject-first and action-first: what happened, what's needed, by when.
- Short paragraphs; concrete verbs; explicit asks and deadlines.
- ID: "Draft laporan sudah saya unggah. Mohon review bagian metodologi sebelum Jumat."
- EN: "I've uploaded the report draft. Could you review the methodology section by Friday?"
- Watch: bureaucratic padding, passive-aggressive softening, buzzwords ("sinergi", "solusi end-to-end").

## 4. Kasual
**Use for**: blog posts, social media, community newsletters, friendly announcements.
- Contractions; relaxed connectives; 1st/2nd person; colloquialisms that a real speaker of that audience would actually use.
- ID: "Jadi begini ceritanya — kami baru pindah kantor, dan ternyata internetnya nggak secepat yang dikira."
- EN: "So here's the thing — we just moved offices, and the internet isn't as fast as promised."
- Watch: borrowed slang you wouldn't say aloud, emoji spam, pretending to be younger/cooler than the author is.
- Casual is a register, not a license for typos or grammar errors.

## 5. Diplomatis
**Use for**: sensitive feedback, negotiation, refusals, incident communication, conflict.
- Soften the directive, keep the content honest: "Mungkin kita bisa pertimbangkan..." / "Perhaps we could consider...".
- Frame around shared interest and next steps; questions over accusations.
- ID: "Terima kasih untuk draftnya. Ada beberapa bagian yang perlu kita selaraskan sebelum final — boleh saya usulkan alternatifnya?"
- EN: "Thanks for the draft. A few sections need alignment before we finalize — may I suggest alternatives?"
- Watch: vagueness that hides the actual message; hedging until nothing is said.
- Hard limit: diplomacy changes framing, not facts — the real problem still gets named.

## 6. Sekolah
**Use for**: high-school/undergraduate assignments, teacher-facing writing, exam practice.
- Clear thesis sentence; explicit argument steps; defined terms; strict standard grammar (EYD/grammar rules apply fully).
- One idea per paragraph; concrete examples; brief restating conclusion.
- ID: "Tesis penulis: sampah plastik di sungai dapat dikurangi lewat tiga langkah — pemilahan, daur ulang, dan edukasi warga."
- EN: "This essay argues that plastic waste in rivers can be reduced through sorting, recycling, and public education."
- Watch: thesaurus words the student can't define, padding for length, invented "facts" or citations to look smart.
- Integrity line: never fabricate sources/citations for schoolwork — refuse that part, explain why, and keep helping with the rest.

## 7. Simple
**Use for**: public information, instructions, broad-audience summaries, accessibility-focused text.
- Short sentences (target ≤15 words), one idea each; common words; active voice; concrete nouns.
- Jargon defined once or removed; lists only when they aid scanning.
- ID: "Cuci tangan dengan sabun selama 20 detik. Lakukan setelah dari toilet dan sebelum makan."
- EN: "Wash your hands with soap for 20 seconds. Do this after using the toilet and before eating."
- Watch: condescension; losing precision while simplifying — a simplified claim must stay true.

## 8. Percaya Diri
**Use for**: pitches, cover letters, proposals, opinion pieces where authority matters.
- Active voice; decisive verbs ("akan", "will"); recommendations without throat-clearing; ownership ("saya memimpin", "I led").
- ID: "Saya memimpin migrasi sistem ini, selesai dua minggu lebih awal, dengan nol downtime."
- EN: "I led the system migration, finished two weeks early, with zero downtime."
- Watch: fake certainty — confidence lives in the phrasing, never beyond the evidence. No "proven/guaranteed/terbukti" without proof, no invented numbers. This preset is the top source of résumé/CIK-style hallucinations: only claims the user can back.

## 9. Marketing
**Use for**: landing pages, product copy, promo emails, ads, campaign content.
- Reader-focused ("kamu/Anda/you"); benefit-first (feature → what it does for the reader); hook in line one; scannable blocks; explicit CTA.
- Energy allowed; hype banned. Concrete benefit beats superlative.
- ID: "Bayangkan pulang kerja tanpa nyuci piring. Cuci piring kami selesai dalam 10 menit — kamu tinggal bilas."
- EN: "Imagine coming home to an empty sink. Our dishwasher finishes in 10 minutes — you just rinse."
- Watch: empty hype ("revolusi", "terbaik di dunia"), fabricated testimonials/stats/reviews (absolute red line), fake urgency.
- Health/finance claims get the same verification as any factual claim.

## Selection protocol (summary)

1. Named style → lock it.
2. Unambiguous context → infer + state in the change log.
3. Ambiguous + stakes → one question, 2–3 options.
4. Always: presets never override meaning lock, anti-hallucination, or grammar gates; scan Marketing and Percaya Diri output extra carefully for AI tells (highest density risk).
